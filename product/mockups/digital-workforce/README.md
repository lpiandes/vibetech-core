# Digital Workforce Mockups

## Intended layout

### Reading order (top to bottom)
1. **Digital Workforce header**
   - The page immediately answers: **“How is my Digital Workforce doing?”**
2. **Workforce summary strip**
   - One calm block that communicates: workforce state, review demand, and a short timing estimate.
3. **Employee cards list**
   - A stacked set of cards, one per **Digital Employee**.
   - Each card is structured as a “handoff card” from employee to manager.
4. **Secondary actions**
   - Links for deeper context that do not compete with the main decision moment.

### Relationship to Employee Profile
- **Employee Profile** is for deeper understanding of an individual Digital Employee.
- **Digital Workforce** is for making today’s manager decision quickly across multiple employees.
- The summary and cards should reference profile context indirectly (role, specialty, recent work signals) without duplicating the full profile.

## Emotional experience

The page should feel like you are managing a team:
- You are not hunting through records.
- You are receiving structured, human-readable updates from your employees.
- You always know what needs your attention now and what is safe to leave untouched.

### What users should feel first
- Trust: the work is explained in managerial language.
- Calm: the page has a governed, consistent rhythm.
- Control: “Waiting on you” clarifies whether you need to review.

## Card behavior philosophy (documentation-only)

Each employee card should emphasize:
- identity (who this is),
- current status (what they are doing),
- today’s accomplishments (proof of progress),
- a single quality signal (approval rate),
- and the primary decision CTA (do they need you?).

Metadata (like workload breakdowns) is supportive, never louder than the manager’s decision moment.
